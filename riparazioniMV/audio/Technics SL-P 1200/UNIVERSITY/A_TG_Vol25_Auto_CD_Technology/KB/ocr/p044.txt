| 1) Principle of the Digital Filter

The way an audio wave is converted to a
digital signal, processed digitally, and
then returned toa high-purity audio
form, is more and more widely
understood.

The conversion from analogue into
digital signals is often called pulse
code modulation, or PCM. The analogue
waveform is first sampled (measured at
regular intervals). Then it is
quantized. This means that it is given
the nearest numerical value in the large
range of numbers used to transmit the
digital sound information. The quantized
value is then converted to binary form
and encoded into a pulse train
representing the series of numbers. This
process of analogue’ to digital
conversion is shows in figure 5-1.

Reversing the process produces a replica
of the original signal. It is in this
second stage that CD player use digital
filtering to achieve exceptionally
accurate reproduction.

The pulse train is fed into’ the
transmission system ( a cable or radio
link, a tape, or adisc etc). At the
reception or playback end, the pulse
train is detected, decoded and
regenerated in analogue form in the
reverse process known as digital to
analogue conversion.

2) How the Number of Bits Affects S/N

The accuracy of a digital system depends
on the number of bits used for the
binary number. This is because the
number of bits determines the accuracy
with which the samples taken from the
Original waveform can be quantized, i.e.
expressed in digital form.

Two bits give four 2 possible values for
use in quantization, also three bits
give eight 2 possible values.

Every bit added doubles the number of
possible values. In audio terms, this
adds 6dB to the signal to noise ratio,
because it reduces the quantization
noise by a factor of 2.

The sixteen bits as used in the CD
system give 65536 (216 ) possible values
for quantization, anda signal to noise
ratio of 96dB. The audio signals are
encoded in the actual CD with this
ratio.

960B is approximation. An additional
factor, which depends on the type of
Signal, is always present, regardless of
the number of bits; for sine wave
signals this makes the -theoretical
figure 97.8dB.

EFM is a new modulation system for CD, and stands for Eight to Fourteenth Modulation meaning
modulating 8 symbol data bits to 14 channel bits.

EFM is necessary because the data bit arrangement of 1's and O’s cannot be defined if symbol data
remains as such.

If 1’s or O's continue so long, data will be a direct current so that self-clocks cannot be generated by
the tracking servo (using pits) and reproducing system.

Conversely, if 1’s and O's are frequently reversed, it will be necessary to reduce the laser beam to a
very small diameter.

Therefore, signals to be recorded must be such that 1‘s and O's are reversed at appropriate intervals
and that the DC component is as small as possible.

8 bits has 256 combinations (2°), while 14 bits has 16,384 combinations (2!*).

EFM selects bit patterns, in which 2 to 10 O's are present between two 1's, from among 2!* combi-
nations for correspondence to 2° data patterns.

Therefore, signals to be recorded have definitions with 3T to 11T (T meaning 1, 1 clock after EFM
= 4.32 MHz).

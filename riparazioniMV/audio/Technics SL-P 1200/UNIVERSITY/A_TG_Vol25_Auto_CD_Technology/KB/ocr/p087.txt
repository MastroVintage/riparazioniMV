The CLV servo is a rotation speed control servo for maintaining linear speed of the disk constant.
The servo is divided into three parts, that is 11T servo, CLV servo, and traverse tracking servo. The
traverse tracking servo and 11T servo are rough servos, and the CLV servo is a fine servo. The CLV
control which is the primary part of the circuit in the LSI. The CLV servo circuits consists of micro-
computers AN8371S and AN8281S.

11T servo

The 11t servo changes the constant speed to constant linear speed.

This requires some signal extracted from the disk, that is, SRF signal. (SRF signal is generated
by data-slicing RF signal.)

The 11T servo controls the SRF signals that the longest period of the signal will be equivalent 11
times the period of the reference clock 1T (4.3 MHz).

When the 11T servo operates, the spindle motor comes close to the control speed of the fine
servo.

CLV servo

The CLV servo is started by the 11T servo when digital signals are ready for processing in the
LSI.

For digital signal processing, data is once written in an external RAM and read out again. A
RAM address generator circuit is provided in the LSI for this purpose. Because a crystal clock
is used for reading data and a clock synchronized with reproducing signals (corresponding to
PCK) is used for writing data, the address locations fluctuates in RAM. The fine servo detects
the address location signals and operates to keep the read/wirte pace constant.

Traverse tracking servo

The traverse tracking servo follows the pick-up and controls the tinear speed of the pick-up
constant when the traverse mechanism at high speed,

This servo operates in such a way that two continuous minimum periods of the SRF will be 6
times the period of the reference clock 1T (4.3 MHz).

TRON is used for switching the traverse tracking servo to the 11T servo or vice versa. That is,
the traverse tracking servo is selected when TRON is high and the 11T servo is selected when
TRON is low.

Introduction

The widespread popularity of Hi-Fi Digital Audio has made the compact disc player a
common place component in today’s audio systems. Fortunately, in the years since its
initial introduction, the serviceability of the compact disc player has improved
dramatically, mostly due to advances in digital technology. What this technology was not
capable of doing however, is to do away with the various mechanical and electrical
adjustments required on these units.

This guide is meant to eliminate trial-and-error servicing procedures and aid in the
repairing of compact disc players by standardizing the procedure. If followed correctly,
the optimum operating condition of the unit can be achieved by using this guide. For this
reason, it is important that all of the procedures listed in this guide be performed
BEFORE replacing any optical pickup assemblies.

Included along with this procedure is a useful reference chart containing pickup part
numbers and related information.

NOTE: THE FOLLOWING PROCEDURES CONTAIN GENERAL INFORMATION.
FOR SPECIFIC DETAILS PLEASE REFER TO THE CORRESPONDING
SERVICE MANUAL FOR YOUR UNIT.

Models
All compact disc players from 1986 to 1990.

Possible Symptoms

1. Skipping.

2. TOC is read, but disc does not play.
3. No TOC.

4. Poor playability.

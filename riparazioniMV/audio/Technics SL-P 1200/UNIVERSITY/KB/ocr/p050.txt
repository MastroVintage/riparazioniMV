The CLV servo is a rotary servo which keeps disk linear velocity constant.

The servo is divided into three parts, that is 11T servo, CLV servo, and traverse tracking servo. The
traverse tracking servo and 11T servo are rough servos, and the CLV servo is a fine servo. The CLV
control which is the nucleus of the circuit is in the LSI. The CLV servo circuit consists of micro-
computers AN8371S and AN8281S.

11T servo

The 11T servo brings disk rotation close to constant linear velocity.

This requires a signal extracted from the disk, that is, SRF signal. (SRF signal is generated by
data-slicing RF signal.)

The 11T servo operates in such a way that the longest period of SRF signal will be 11 times the
period of the reference clock 1T (4.3 MHz).

When the 11T servo operates, the spindle motor comes close to the rotating speed of the fine
servo.

CLV servo

The CLV servo is started by the 11T servo when digital signals are ready for processing in the
LSI.

For digital signal processing, data is once written in an external RAM and read out again. A
RAM address generator circuit is provided in the LSI for this purpose. Because a crystal clock
is used for reading data and a clock synchronized with reproducing signals (corresponding to
PCK). is used for writing data, however, address locations may be unsteady relative to RAM
capacity. The fine servo detects address location signals and operates to keep the read/write pace
constant.

Traverse tracking servo

The traverse tracking servo tracks the traverse position to keep linear velocity constant when
the traverse mechanism is in rapid feed.

This servo operates in such a way that two consecutive shortest period of SRF will be 6 times
the period of the reference clock 1T (4.3 MHz).

TRON is used for switching the traverse tracking servo to the 11T servo or vice versa. That is,
the traverse tracking servo is selected when TRON is high; the 11T servo is selected when TRON
is low.

1. Analog VS Digital

1-1. Quality

Analog recordings have undergone
remarkable improvements to data, but
they are still limited by distortion and
in dynamic range. Digital recording, on
the other hand, is a_ revolutionary

technique which eliminates these
limitations.
Digital disc typically have a flat

frequency response throughout the entire
audible frequency range of 20 through
20,000 Hz. Their wide dynamic range
exceeds 90dB, as the digital signal can
always be recovered and is identical to
the original signal. Totally different
from present analog turntable system,
the digital discs never exhibit
deterioration in sound reproduction
quality no matter how many times it is
played. Even more remarkably, they can
include flexible, convenient automatic
play and program search features that
were never possible with conventional
analog player designs.

1-2. Advantages of Optical System

a) Exceptional Durability

With the mechanical system as in the

current analog player system, the stylus
must be in physical with the disc.

Therefore both the stylus and the disc
will wear out after a certain period of
time, causing serious deterioration of
sound quality.

With the optical system, however , the
lack of contact between the disc and the
pick-up means that there is no sonic
deterioration no matter how many times
the disc is played.

b) Harmful Effect of Dust and Scratches
Minimized

Mechanical and variable capacitance
system are easily affected by dust and
scratches, since signals are impressed
directly in the disc surface. The
compact disc, on the other hand, has a
Imm thick protective layer aver tha
signal pits. More importantly, the spot
size of the laser beam is only about 1mm
in diameter at the surface of the disc,
but is as small as lnm at the signal
layer. This means that a dust particle
or a scratch on the disc surface is
actually only a millionth in size on the
signal surface.

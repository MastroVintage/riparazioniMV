1) Single Beam System

The single beam detection system
includes the push-pull,
phasedifference, heterodyne, and

others. The push-pull type treats the
pits string on the disc as a_ single
track and detects tracking error from
variation of the far-field pattern of
the diffracted ray, whereas the phase-
difference and heterodyne types detect
the variation of far-field patterns of
the beam defracted from individual
pits.

The push pull type requires a_ simpler
optical system than the three beam
technique, but is sensitive to the
disc's tilt angle or pit depth and the
error signal is affected by the focus
lens position. For these reasons, it
is rarely used for practical
application.

The phase difference and heterodyne
typess eliminate the drawbacks found
with the push pull technique. While
the optical system is similar to that
used in the push pull technique
(except for a 4-split photodetector),
they have different circuit
configurations.

The single beam technique requires a
servo circuit a little more complex
than that required for the three beam
system, but depending on servo circuit
design provides better playability
than the latters.

2) 2 Dimension Actuator

The 2-D actuator is the only movable
component of the optical pickup. It
requires precision design because it
drives the focusing lens and same
other pickup components requiring
extreme positional accuracy. Recent 2-
D actuators include three types: 4
wire, rotary, and swing arm types. In
here will explain the 4 wire actuator
as follows;

4 Wire Actuator:

The 4 wire actuator uses four metallic
wires to suspend a moving coil bobbin.
When the coil is energized, the wires
are slack, which lets the coil bobbin
move in the focusing and tracking
directions. The main point is to drive
the bobbin's center of gravity and
this gives the four wires and even
deformation and keeps the optical axis
of the lens straight.

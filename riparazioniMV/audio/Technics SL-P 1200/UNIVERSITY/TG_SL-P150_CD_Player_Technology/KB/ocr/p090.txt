4) Quantization Noise

In a digital audio system the audible
noise has a different character to the
familiar noise in an analogue audio
system. The digital audio system
background noise is called quantization
noise.

The quantization noise of a system can
be defined as the error signal between
the original waveform before the
analogue to digital conversion process,
and the restored waveform after the
digital to analogue conversion process.
This error signal can be considered as
both noise and distortion at the same
time.

Figure 19-3

Figure 19-3 shows the quantization
noise, The difference between the
original audio waveform = and the

regenerated wave after D/A conversion
(a) causes quantization noise (b).

The 16 bits analogue to digital
conversion at the recording’ stage
delivers a 16 bits digital signal with
an intrinsic accuracy or signal to noise
ration of maximum 96dB.

An ideal digital to analogue converter
(D/A) would deliver an analogue output
signal without adding noise to it.
However, ideal D/A do not exist a small
but finite electronic inaccuracy adds
noise to the quantization noise of the
digital signal.

The inaccuracy of a 16 bits D/A (the
resolution limit of half the least

significant bit) can be interpreted as a
noise source that reduces the signal to
noise ratio of the D/A to 96dB.

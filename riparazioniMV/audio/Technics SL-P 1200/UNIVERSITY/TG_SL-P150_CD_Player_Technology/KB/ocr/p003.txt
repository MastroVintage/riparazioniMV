3. Compact Disc Features

1) Superior Sound Features

To maintain master recorder quality,
the information on the disc is recorded
with 16 bits linear quantization.

To improve recording density (playback
time) eight to fourteen bits modulation
(EFM) is used. This allows 2. channel
playback for max 74 minutes.

To realize stable data reproduction a
special error correcting code called
the Cross Interleave Reed-Solomon Code
(CIRC) is used.

2) Virtually Indestructible

The non contact laser playback system
allows repeated use with no degradation
in quality.

The plastic coating over the signal
information protects it against
scratches, dirt and fingerprints.

3) Small Size
The small size allows for easy compact
strage (12cm and 8cm CD).

4) Random Access

The control information recorded on the
disc allows random access and character
display such as names of tunes, artists
etc.

4. PCM Features

Using a PCM system for audio

reproduction has certain advantages.

1)Not affected by noise of the recording
medium, The digital system only has
detect two types of signals (0 and 1).
This is much easier than trying to
detect the infinite number of possible
amplitudes and frequencies of an
analog signal.

2)Eliminates effects of uneven rotation
of the playback equipment due to a
quartz controlled reading clock.

3)Able to correct errors, besides
including the recorded signal, the

digital data has error correction
information. This allows the digital
circuit to calculate what the

information should be if the music
signal can not be rear properly
because the disc surface is marked by
dirt or scratches etc.

5. PCM Process for CD

The PCM process has several fundamental

steps regardless of whether the
application is satelite data
transmission or simply audio

reproduction etc. A general description
is given of the recording and playback
procedures followed by an explanation of
each steps.

6. Disc Mastering

6-1 Campact Disc Cutting

In a Compact Disc Cutting System, the
Digital Audio signal is sent through a
CIRC encording modulator unit. In this
way, EFM modulation is performed and
control and sync bits are added. The
modulated signal is then sent to a laser
cutter to produce the metal master.

The cutting of the metal master is not
really a burning of the layer by the
laser. In figure 6-1 the real cutting is
represented.

Step 1:
A glass plate is polished for optimum
smoothness of the surface.

Step 2:

The photo-resist liquid is coated on
the surface. Theroughness of the glass
surface and the thickness of the coating
determine the depth of the pits on the
compact disc.

Step 3:

The photo-resistive coating is then,
exposed to a laser beam, modulated with
digitized audio information.

16. Optical Pickup

1. Basic Operation of the Laser Diode

The basic operation of any laser
consists of pumping the atoms into a
stimulated state from which the
electrons can escape and fall to the
lower energy ground state by giving up a
photo of the appropriate energy. In a
solid-state laser such as the laser
diodes used in CD player, this principle
is applied to a PN-junction, as will be
explained below.

diagrams as shows figure 16-1.

As is commonly known, the energy state
of electrons falls within a certain
range or energy band.

The energy bands required for electrical

conductivity are:

(a)The "valency band", indicating the
energy level of the valency electron
and:

(b)The "conduction band", indicating the
energy level of the free electron.

In a metal, the valency band and

conduction band overlap each other in

part, so that the valency electron can
enter the conduction band and move
freely, which causes conductivity.

In a semiconductor or insulator, there
is an energy gap between the valency
band and the conduction band; this band
is called the forbidden band. In case of
an insulator, the forbidden or energy
gap "Eg" is large.

For a semiconductor, the energy gap is
rather small, so that it can become a
conductor by applying some external
energy.

P-type and N-type semiconductors can be
made by inserting impurities into the
semiconductor crystal. In the P-type
semiconductor, positive holes are
generated, which can move freely within
the valency band, and in the N-type
semiconductor, electrons are generated
which can move freely in the conduction
band.

If both types of semiconductors are
combined into one crystal, i.e. in a PN
junction, a natural electrical field is
created in this PN-junction section, but
the free electrons and positive holes
are kept in the respective areas, and do
not move to the other area, as they do
not have enough energy to cross the Eg
level. However, by applying energy from
outside, e.g. a voltage or current, to
the PN junction, the electrons and
positive holes start moving into the
other area as showns figure 16-2. Asa

result, electrons and positive holes,
coexisting in the PN junction, are now
recombined, and natural light is

emitted.

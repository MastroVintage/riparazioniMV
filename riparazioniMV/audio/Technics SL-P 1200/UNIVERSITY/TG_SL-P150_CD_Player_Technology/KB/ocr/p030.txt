1. Analog to Digital Conversion

2. CIRC Encoding

6 words (of 16 bits) of the L-channel
A/D converter and 6 words (of 16 bits)
of the R-channel A/D converter are fed
to the "C2" encoder. These 12 words of
16 bits are broken into 24 words of 8
bits. This is the input of the encoder

The "C2" encoder adds 4 words of 8
bits, and its output is 28 words of 8
bits. The :C2" encoder is called a
(24, 28) encoder. and The "C1" encoder
receives these 28 words at the input
and adds again 4 words of 8 bits. Its
output is 32 words of 8 bits and the
"C1" encoder is called a (28, 32)
encoder.

The extra words ( 8 words x 8 bit = 64
bits ) are called redundant words and
are used for the error correction.

The new bit rate now equals:
1.411200 x 32/24 = 1.881600 MHz

3. The Control Word

One control word ( 1 word x 8 bits) is
added to every 32 words of the
encoder.

The new bit rate becomes:
1.881600 x 33/32 = 1.940400 MHz

4. EFM Encoeding

Every word of 8 bits is replaced by a
"symbol" of 14 bits. To thaca 14 bite
3 extra junction bits are added. The
resultant operation transforms 8 data
bits into 17 (14 + 3) channel bits.

The new bit rate is:
1.940400 x 17/8 = 4.123350 MHz

5. The Sync Word

The 33 symbols of 17 bits (= 561 bits)
are preceded by one sync word (24
bits) and its 3 junction bits. Every
561 bits are complemented with 27 (24
+ 3) bits, giving 588 bits in total.
These 588 bits make one "frame".

The final bit rate (on the CD) become:
4.123350 x 588/561 = 4.321800 MHz

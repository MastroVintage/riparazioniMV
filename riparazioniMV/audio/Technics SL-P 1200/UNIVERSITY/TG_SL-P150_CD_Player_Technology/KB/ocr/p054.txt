(1) Autofocus Circuit

The autofocus circuit is operated by the
AF ON (autofocus command) signal output
from the control logic circuit. Two
signals (RF detection signal and S-curve
detection signal) are input to the
autofocus circuit which regulates the
search switch and FE (focus error)
switch.

1 At detection of the disc

On the receipt of the AF ON
(autofocus on) signal, at first the
search switch is turned on and next
the focus error switch is turned off.
In this situation, the search signal
(sawtooth waves) drives the focus
coil forcedly to move the lens up and
down.

When the distance between the disc
and the lens nears the focus length
(lock-in focus range), the FE signal
shows an "S" curve, with the search
switch is turned off and the focus
error switch is turned on to close
the focus servo loop, as shows in
figure 17-9.

2 Re-locking in focus

The autofocus circuit checks the
servo status by RF detection when the
focus servo loop is closed. If the
disc deviates from the focal point
and RF search oscillation is started.
The search oscillation signal is
input to the system CPU. Whether or
not autofocus is working is known by
the voltage at the pin 26 (FLOCK) of
Ic-101. The voltage level is active
"L" when locked in focus.

(2) Focus Error Circuit

The sums of output signals from pair of
diagonal segments each (A1+A2) and
(A3+A4) of the 4 segment PD are applied
to the pin 39 and 42 of Ic-101. The I/V
converter converts the output currents
of PD to voltages.

The gain is set by an exterior resistor,
and VR101 is the best eye adjuster for
setting off the sensitivity difference
of PD.

The difference between the converted two
signal (A1+A2) -— (A3+A4) is focus error
signal (FE). The FE signal applied to
the pin 36 (FE OUT) of IC-101 via the FE
amplifier and FE switch, and applied to
the pin 3 of IC-104 (A).

VR104 is the adjuster of focus gain (FO
GAIN) and VR105 is the adjuster of focus
off set (FO OFFSET).

The FE signal applied to the focus drive
circuit from pin 36 of IC-101, The first
subjected to phase compensation by
R142,R141, and cCl4l. It then goes
through a low-pass filter comprised of
IC-104 (B), R144, C142, and C143, and
then an amplifier (with low frequency

compensation) comprised of  IC-106,
R149, C144, and R148.

The output of the amplifier is applied
to current-feedback focus drive, Q141
and Q142, which then drives the focusing
lens drive coil as required, as shows in
figure 17-10.

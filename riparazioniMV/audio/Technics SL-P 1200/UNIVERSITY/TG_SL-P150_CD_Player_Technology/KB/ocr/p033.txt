2. Opto-Electronics

The technological marriage of the fields
of optics and electronics is called
"opto-electronic". It is a remarkable
diverse field involving such topics at

the nature of optical radiation, the
interaction of light with matter,
radiometry, photometry and the

characteristics of various sorces and
sensors.

1) The Optical Spectrum
By convention, electromagnetic
radiation is generally = specified
according to its metric wavelength

(x). The frequency of a specific
electromagnetic wavelenght can be
found from:

f = c/d

Where f is frequency in Hz:

The optical portion of the
electromagnetic spectrum extends from
10 nm (100 A) to 10°nm and is divided
into three major categories:
ultraviolet (UV), visible and
infrared (IR).

Ultraviolet are those wavelengths,
falling below the visible spectrum
and above x-rays.

UV is classified according to its
wavelength as extreme or short wave
UV (10 - 200 nm), far (200 - 300 nm)
ane near or long wave UV (300 - 370
nm).

Visible are those wavelengths between
370 - 750 nm and they can be
perceived by the human eye. Visible
light is classified according to the
various colours its wavelengths
elicit in the mind of a_ standard
observer.

The major colour categories. are:
violet (370 - 455 nm), blue (456
492 nm), green (493 - 577 nm), yellow
(578 - 597 nm), orange (598 - 622 nm)
and red (623 - 750 nm).

Infrared (IR) are those wavelengths
above the visible spectrum and below
microwaves. IR is classified
according to its wavelength as near
(750 -1500 nm) middle (1600 -6000 nm)
, far (6100 - 40000 nm) and far-far
(41000 - 10 nm).

2) Optical Waves with Matter
An optical wave may interact with
matter by being reflected, refracted,
absorbed or transmitted. The
interaction normally involves two or
more of the these effects.

Some of the optical radiation
impinging upon any surface is
reflected away from the — surface.

reflectance varies according to the

properties of the surface and the
wavelength and in real circumstances
may range from more than 98% to less

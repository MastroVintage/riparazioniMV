2. Quantization Error or Noise

in fact, quantization can be regarded as
a mechanism in which some information is
thrown away, keeping only as much as is
required to retain a certain accuracy as
is needed in a certain application.

By definition, since all voltages in a
certain quantization interval are
represented by the voltage at the center
of this interval, the process of
quantization is a non-linear process and
creates anerror, called quantization
error.

This error can be perceived s a kind of
noise, and therefore the quantization
error is also called quantization noise.
It is clear that, the smaller the
quantization intervals, the smaller this
quantization noise will be.

With a 16 bits system, s&s used in
Compact Disc, a theoretic 98dB S/N ratio
possible as following calculation:

S/N (dB) = 6.02 x n +1.76

* n = number of bits

11. Architecture of D/A Converter

1. Introduction

Obviously, one of the most important
components in a D/A conversion system
is the converter itself. From the
foregoing it has become clear that
very high resolution is needed to
obtain a satisfactory signal to noise
ratio.

Compact Disc system use a 16 bits
resolution (compared toe.g. 8 bits
for digital video applications).

2. D/A Converters

The principle of the best known, so
called weighted current, D/A converter
is shown in figure 11-1.

Each bit of the input word controls a
switch in the circuit that turns on or
off aspecified current. The problem
in this apparently simple circuit is
the number of precision resistors
needed : an n-bit converter requires n
precision resistors overall : 2n-l
value range.

Figure 11-1 Weighted Current D/A
Converter

Also the required accuracy in the
resistors for the nighest bits is
extremely high as the accuracy of its
current switched by the LSB (which, in
a 16 bits converter, is 1/32,768 of
the current switched by the MSB; this
means an accuracy better than 0.003%).
Also, this accuracy must be maintained
over a long periode of time and
temperature range.

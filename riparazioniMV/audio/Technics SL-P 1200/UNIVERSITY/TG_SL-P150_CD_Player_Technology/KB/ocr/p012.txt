10-2 Sample Hold Circuit

In the practice of analog to digital
conversion, the sampling operation is
performed by so called sample hold
circuit which, once the sample has been
taken, store the sample analog voltage
for the time during which it can be
converted into a digital code by the A/D
converter, The principle of the sample
hold circuit is relatively simple.

A basic sample hold circuit is in fact a
voltage .memory device that stores a
given voltage in a high-quality
capacitor. To sample the input voltage,
switch S closes momentarily; when §
opens, capacitor C holds the voltage
until S closes again and passes the next
sample, see figure 10-5,

The sample hold circuit are not only
used in A/D conversion to sample an
analog input signal, but also in the
reverse stage, after the D/A conversion,
to remove transients from the output of
the D/A converter and thus facilitate
the task of the low pass filter. In this
case, it is often called deglitcher.

10-3 Filtering

In the foregoing, we have seen that low
pass filtering is required at two places
in a digital audio system:

1) During recording, in the A/D stage
before the input sampler, we need
an anti-aliasing filter that
drastically suppresses all input
frequencies higher than the Nyquist

frequency.

2) During playback, i.e. in the compact
disc player, we need a
reconstruction filter after the

sample hold in the D/A stage to
separate the sample audio signal
from its images sround the harmonics
of the sampling frequency.

Consequently, both filters must be very
linear within the passband, and have a
very high rejection out of it; this will
unavoidably bring along phase and group
delay distortions within the audio
passband as well as ripple and ringing.
Apart from that, the noise and THD
performance of these filters has to be
better than the limits posed by the
quantization process itself. In practice
this means that 100dB S/N ratio have to
be attained.

For the practical realization of such
low pass filter, there are serveral
approaches possible:

1) Passive Filter:

7th to 9th order filters must be used
to obtain the required rejection. The
coils which must be used give
problemss because of non-linearity,
size andcost. Noise, however, is
hardly a problem in this type of
filters.

2) Active Filter:
Can also be used but the
specifications are more stringent
than usually encountered with active-
filter designs; therefore, a very
careful design is necessary. The
distortion can be made better than

with passive filters, but the noise
worse.

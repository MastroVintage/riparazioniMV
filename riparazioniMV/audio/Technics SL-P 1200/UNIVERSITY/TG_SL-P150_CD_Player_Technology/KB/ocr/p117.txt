6 Apparent Fault

The changer’s auto changing system may exhibit the following malfunctions but they may not be

derived from mechanism or logic failure:

Program numbers which do not exist
on the discs can be programmed in
the Program mode but nothing is
played in Play mode. (e.g. the
25th track was programmed for a

CD containing only 12 tracks.)

Disc trays were loaded only in
slots 1, 2, and 5 in the disc
magazine, and the player was
turned off when it was playing
Disk 5. When the player was
subsequently turned on, Disc 5 was
returned to the tray for Disk 3
(disk position has changed in

the magazine).

Possible cause

Other CD players first read the TOC just after a disc is loaded, th*n enter the
Pause mode to wait for the next command. They can verify the valid-ty of the
programmed contents by comparing the number of tracks on the disc wth that in
the program. However, the SL-P600C takes so much time to read the [OCs on all
of the six discs that it ignores invalid track numbers in the progrim,

As long as the player is left turned on, the CPU memorizes which slot the disc
now on the playing deck came from. However, once the player has been turned
off, the CPU loses its memory and cannot determine to which slot the disc
should be returned. In such a case, the CPU sequentially searches for a vacant
slot from bottom to top, and returns the disc into the first vacant Slot found.
At an occupied slot, the Load switch remains off. The CPU checks the time
until the Load switch is turned on after a return sequence was started (Draw
switch S453 was turned off). If it is too long (more than ms), the CPU
identifies that the slot is occupied.

* This problem can be avoided if all of the six trays are always loaded in the
magazine (discs need not be loaded in all of the six slots), or if trays are
loaded from slot 1 with no intermediate vacant slot left in the magazine.
